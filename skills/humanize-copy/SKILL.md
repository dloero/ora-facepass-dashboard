# Humanize Copy

## Why AI copy sounds like AI

A model writes to be safe with every possible reader. A person writes to be understood by one.

Every AI tell is a safety move. The hedge protects against being wrong. The list of three protects against leaving something out. "Not X, but Y" protects against being misread. The closing line protects against the point being missed. Bold and headers protect against the skim. The balanced tone protects against offending anyone.

Human copy takes a position, names something specific, and leaves things out. So the job is not to swap words. The job is to remove the safety moves and put the specific back.

## Before you touch the draft

Answer three questions from the draft and any context you have:

1. Who is the one reader? A name or a role. Not "prospects." For a page or an ad, pick one real person you have met who fits the segment and write to them.
2. What is the one thing they should believe after reading?
3. What is the one thing they should do?

If the draft cannot answer these, edit anyway and add one bracketed line at the end saying what you assumed.

Never add a fact. A name, number, date, price, quote, or result must come from the draft or from the writer. If the copy needs one and you do not have it, write `[NEED: specific here]` and keep going.

## The pass, in order

Do all seven. Do not skip to the word list. The words are the weakest tell.

### 1. Kill the staging

Staging is anything that announces a point instead of making it.

- "It's not about X, it's about Y." State Y.
- "Let's dive in." "Here's the thing." "Here's what most people miss." Delete. Start with the point.
- A short dramatic closer that repeats the paragraph. "That's the real win." Delete.
- A rhetorical question the writer then answers. Cut the question. Keep the answer.
- Arguing with an objection nobody raised. "This isn't about replacing your team." Delete unless the reader actually said it.
- A saying that sounds deep. "At its core, trust is everything." Replace with the specific claim or delete.

### 2. Break the rhythm rules

AI writes to a metronome. People do not.

- Lists of three. Use the number of items the point needs. Two is fine. Four is fine.
- Mirrored sentences. "Fast to set up. Easy to run. Simple to scale." Break the mirror.
- The same opening word on back-to-back sentences. Change one.
- Same-length sentences. Read the word counts. If three in a row are within five words of each other, cut one to under six words or merge two.
- Em dashes (`—`). Zero in anything under 300 words. Use a period or a comma.
- Semicolons. Zero. Use a period.
- A colon used as a reveal. "The problem: nobody calls back." Make it a sentence.

### 3. Deflate

- Cut every word on the kill list below.
- Cut stacked hedges. "Could potentially help" becomes "helps" or gets cut. Keep one hedge only where the writer is actually unsure.
- Cut inflated significance. "A pivotal moment for your portfolio" is a fact wearing a costume. Keep the fact.
- Cut borrowed authority. "Experts agree" and "studies show" with no source. Name the source or drop the claim.
- Use "is," "has," and "does." Not "serves as," "boasts," "features," "offers a robust."
- Name who does the thing. "Access is granted" becomes "the manager unlocks it."

### 4. Make it checkable

Every claim gets one test: could the reader check this?

- An abstract benefit ("saves time") becomes a number, a name, a date, a place, or a before-and-after, taken from the source. "Cuts a rekey from a locksmith visit to a 2-minute reset."
- Ask "so what?" after every sentence. If the sentence cannot answer, cut it or attach the answer.
- Proof beats adjectives. One real result beats three descriptors. If there is no proof, say less.
- If more than half the draft cannot be made specific, the draft was mostly filler. Say so in one bracketed line and ask for specifics. Do not pad.

### 5. Put a person in it

- Take a position. One opinion the writer would actually defend. No "on one hand, on the other hand."
- First person where a person would use it. Contractions in anything conversational.
- One detail only the writer would know. Something they saw, heard, or tried.
- One ask. If the draft asks for two things, cut to one.
- Say it the way you would say it across a table. If you would not say the sentence out loud to this reader, rewrite it until you would.

### 6. Strip the formatting

- No bold used as decoration. No headers in anything shorter than a document.
- No bullets in a text, DM, or email under 150 words. Turn them into sentences.
- No emoji unless the reader uses them first.
- No horizontal rules, arrows, or labeled lists ("Benefit: ...").
- No chatbot residue. "Great question," "I hope this helps," "Absolutely," "Certainly," "Of course."
- No summary line, motivational closer, or P.S. pep talk.
- Straight quotes, not curly.

### 7. Audit by counting, then fix the hits

Rewriting brings tells back at the same rate. So scan the rewritten text literally. Do not rely on memory.

Count and quote every hit:

- Em dashes: must be 0 under 300 words.
- Semicolons: must be 0.
- Lists of three: must be 0 unless the content has exactly three things.
- "Not X but Y" and "more X than Y" frames: must be 0.
- Kill-list words: must be 0.
- Hedges (could, potentially, might, generally, typically, often): keep only the ones the writer would defend.
- Sentences: write out each word count. In anything over 80 words, longest minus shortest must be 15 or more. At least one sentence under 6 words per 150 words.
- Facts: list every name, number, date, and claim in the rewrite. Each one must appear in the original or the writer's notes. If not, remove it.

Fix the hits. Scan once more. Then output.

## Kill list

Tier 1. Always cut. No exceptions.

delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, landscape, journey, unlock, elevate, empower, seamless, seamlessly, robust, streamline, leverage (as a verb), game-changer, game-changing, cutting-edge, navigate (a situation), realm, harness, foster, embark, "at the end of the day," "in today's fast-paced world," "it's worth noting," "it's important to note," "I hope this finds you well," "just checking in," "just wanted to," "circling back," "reaching out," "touching base," "I'd love to," "I'd be happy to," "feel free to," "don't hesitate to," "excited to announce," "thrilled to."

Tier 2. Cut unless the writer's own samples use the word.

furthermore, moreover, additionally, ultimately, essentially, truly, genuinely, actually, very, really, just, comprehensive, holistic, transformative, revolutionary, innovative, dynamic, vibrant, ensure, utilize, "in order to," solution, solutions, synergy, alignment, ecosystem, "best-in-class," "world-class," "state-of-the-art," "next-level," "top-notch," passionate, "deep dive," "thought leader."

Tier 3. Openers and closers that die on sight.

"Hope you're doing well," "I know you're busy, but," "Quick question," "Let's connect," "Looking forward to hearing from you," "Please let me know your thoughts," "Thanks in advance," "To your success," "Cheers to," "Onward."

## Rules specific to sales and marketing copy

- One reader, one promise, one ask. Per message, per page, per section.
- Open with the reader's problem in the reader's words. Not with the writer, the company, or the product.
- The offer is a plain sentence. What they get, what it costs, what happens next.
- Numbers as digits. "4 hours" not "four hours." Price and timeline stated, not hinted.
- The call to action is a sentence a person would say. "Reply with a day that works" beats "Let's connect." "Book 15 minutes here" beats "Learn more."
- No exclamation points. Confidence is flat.
- Subject lines are plain and short. What the email is about. No hooks, no questions, no "quick."
- No "we" bragging. Every "we" sentence must contain something the reader gets.
- Objections get answered with a fact, not with reassurance.

## Length and rhythm targets by channel

- Text or DM: 1 to 4 lines. No greeting unless the relationship needs it. One ask.
- Cold email: under 90 words. First line is the point. Sign with a first name.
- Follow-up: under 50 words. Reference the last touch in the first line. Give a new reason to reply.
- LinkedIn post: first line is the point. No "here's what I learned." No headers. Short paragraphs, uneven lengths.
- Landing page headline: what they get and what they avoid, in under 12 words. No adjective that cannot be checked.
- Ad: one claim, one proof, one ask.
- Script or voicemail: written the way it is said. Fragments are fine.

## Keep these. They are the human part.

- The writer's quirks. Lowercase in texts, a sentence that runs on, a favorite word.
- Dashes, if the writer's own samples use them.
- Typos in texts and DMs. Do not fix them.
- A real hedge where the writer is honestly unsure.
- Mixed feelings. "This worked and I still don't love it."
- One odd aside. One specific memory. One thing that does not fit the structure.
- Anything inside a quotation, a title, or a proper name. Leave it alone.

## Output

Return the rewritten text only. No preamble, no "here's the edited version," no explanation after.

Two exceptions, each as a single bracketed line after a blank line:

- `[Assumed: reader is X, ask is Y]` when the draft did not say.
- `[Needs specifics: the draft was mostly filler. Give me numbers, names, or a result and I will extend it.]` when the rewrite is under half the original length.

If the user asks what changed, give three lines. Not a list of every edit.

## Example

Draft:

> Hi Sarah, I hope this finds you well! I wanted to reach out because we've developed a robust, cutting-edge access solution that's helping property managers streamline operations and elevate the resident experience. It's not just a smart lock, it's a complete platform. I'd love to hop on a quick call to explore how we might be able to help. Looking forward to hearing from you!

Writer's notes: every turnover means a locksmith visit to rekey. The lock resets from the manager's phone in about 2 minutes. The offer is a demo on one of her doors.

Rewrite:

> Sarah, every turnover costs you a locksmith visit before the next resident can move in. Our lock resets from your phone in about 2 minutes, so the door is ready the same day. Want to see it on one of your doors? Reply with a day and I'll come by.

What changed: the greeting and the hype are gone. Every claim came from the notes, nothing was invented. One position, one ask, 50 words.
